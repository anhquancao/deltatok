Without SIGReg, whiten the latents

DeltaTok-flow over a tc1536 max_gap9 no-SIGReg tokenizer, XXL DiT, Waymo. One knob changes: raw z vs per-channel whitening.

Tokenizer (frozen, shared): deltatok_l12_dtok64_tc1536_nozn_maxgap9_vpt1to2_nosigreg — K=64 delta tokens, target_channels=1536 (no bottleneck), z_norm=false, sigreg_weight=0.0

Runs: BSC 44474612 (raw) / 44475204 (whiten), both trained to epoch 100 (101 evals)
Conclusion. Whitening cuts the flow-matching loss 8× (0.85 vs 6.83, last-10-epoch mean) and lets generated geometry keep improving — raymap −35%, pointmap −25%. Raw latents (RMS ≈14) swamp the flow's unit-variance noise, so the model never fits the target and its decoded geometry plateaus from epoch ~10. With SIGReg off, whitening is not optional.

Takeaways

  • Whitening fixes the objective. Flow loss falls 8× (6.83 → 0.85). Same tokenizer, same DiT — the only change is normalizing each latent channel to std 1.
  • Raw latents starve the noise. At RMS ≈14 the flow's unit-variance noise is a rounding error for most t; the target is dominated by scale and the schedule collapses near t=0.
  • Geometry follows the objective. Whitened raymap −35% and pointmap −25%; both keep dropping while raw z plateaus flat and noisy from epoch ~10.
  • Depth is the exception. Both ≈4.1 — the least scale-sensitive channel, so whitening barely moves it.
  • Bottom line. Without SIGReg to shape the latent geometry, whitening is the cheap substitute that makes the flow trainable.
Scope. One tokenizer (tc1536, max_gap9, no SIGReg), one flow arm (XXL DiT, Waymo cam0). This isolates whitening only against no SIGReg — it does not compare whitening against a SIGReg-trained latent, where the geometry is already better conditioned.
