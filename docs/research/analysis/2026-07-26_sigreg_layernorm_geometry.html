SIGReg works — but the z LayerNorm is the wrong geometry for it, and the weight is far too high

The _pool estimator fix held: Train/LossSIGReg descends 0.01615 → 0.00681 over 26k steps and the latent genuinely de-collapses (participation rank 44.6 → 69.4, per-channel std ratio 3.57 → 1.68). But recon pays ~82% on KITTI. Two separate causes, established here: (1) at sigreg_weight=1.0 the regularizer simply outweighs recon — and clipping is not the mechanism (correction below); (2) our non-affine LayerNorm(768) pins every token to ‖z‖=√768, so the radial component of SIGReg's N(0,I) target is structurally unreachable. Neither LeJEPA (bare Linear), DiTo (LN over the whole latent), nor upstream DeltaTok (affine, pre-bottleneck, no SIGReg) imposes that constraint. The LN is not the cause of the rank collapse.

  • LeJEPA's λ does not transfer. Correctly translated, their headline λ=0.05 is sigreg_weight ≈ 81 in our units — 80× above the 1.0 that already broke recon. Our 1.0 is λeff ≈ 0.00065, ~15× below their smallest swept value. The transfer is invalid because SIGReg is LeJEPA's primary objective and our auxiliary one.

Why recon suffers — and why it is not gradient clipping

Correction. An earlier hypothesis in this session was that grad_clip=0.1 lets SIGReg eat the clip budget and starve recon. That is wrong. clip_grad_norm_ (deltatok_trainer.py:958) multiplies the entire gradient vector by a single scalar c = clip/‖g‖; recon and SIGReg components are scaled identically, so the update direction is untouched. Worse for the hypothesis: under AdamW a persistent uniform rescale is close to a no-op, since m scales by c, v by c², leaving m/(√v+ε) unchanged.

The real asymmetries, all visible in the code:

  1. SIGReg reaches only the encoder half. It is computed on z_bneck (deltatok_trainer.py:947), which forward returns as self.norm(z) (line 356 / 451). Backprop runs through self.norm → z_proj_down → pre_bottleneck_norm → encoder blocks and stops. It never reaches z_proj_up or the decoder. Recon flows through everything.
  2. The objectives are directly opposed at z. The z that minimizes recon is anisotropic — that anisotropy is the code. SIGReg pushes toward isotropic Gaussian. At weight 1.0 SIGReg wins, and the probe shows exactly that.
  3. Only 1/grad_cum of the pooled rows carry gradient. Banked micro-batch rows are .detach()ed (deltatok_trainer.py:601–606), so the statistic is computed over grad_cum × world_size × S rows but gradient flows only through the last micro-batch's live rows. Line 953 then scales the weight by grad_cum to compensate — correct for the loss value, but it means the flowing gradient is amplified 2× (grad_cum=2) while sourced from a single micro-batch.

The informative measurement is therefore not total grad norm but the ratio ‖∂Lsigreg/∂z‖ / ‖∂Lrecon/∂z‖ at the bottleneck. Not yet logged.

Reference comparison — three codebases, three different norms

LeJEPA (../lejepa) — no norm at all

self.proj = MLP(512, [2048, 2048, proj_dim], norm_layer=nn.BatchNorm1d)
...

torchvision.ops.MLP puts norm_layer only on the hidden layers. Verified layer stack (torchvision 0.20.0): Linear, BatchNorm1d, ReLU, Dropout, Linear, BatchNorm1d, ReLU, Dropout, Linear, Dropout — the output layer is a bare Linear. SIGReg's input has free mean, free scale, free per-sample norm. BatchNorm1d is per-channel-across-batch anyway, an orthogonal constraint that never touches the output.

Doc bug found. occrae/sigreg.py:25-26 says dropping ×N makes the value "batch-size-independent". It is backwards — the ×N version is the one whose null value is O(1) in N; ours falls as ~1/N, which is precisely the "finite-sample floor ~0.0085 at S=128" the next paragraph of the same docstring describes. No result changes, but it makes the logged Train/LossSIGReg easy to misread across batch shapes.

DiTo (github.com/yinboc/dito) — LN over the whole latent

# models/ldm/ldm_base.py:70-73
self.z_layernorm = nn.LayerNorm(
    list(z_shape),          # configs/experiments/dito-XL-f8c4.yaml:12 -> [4, 32, 32]

normalized_shape is the entire latent tensor — C=4, H=32, W=32 jointly, 4096 values. On a (B,4,32,32) input that is one mean and one std per image. The paper's rationale (Appendix B): LayerNorm replaces the LDM's KL loss, "ensures z₀ to have 0 mean and 1 std so that it does not collapse to the trivial solution", avoids a loss weight to tune, and reconstructs better than KL.

The key point is not magnitude but alignment. Both remove a negligible fraction of dof. But SIGReg's sampling unit is the token, and our LN applies its constraint to exactly that object — 0.26% of total dof, but 100% of the radial dof of every SIGReg sample. DiTo's constraint corresponds to no sampling unit at all; per-position channel vectors keep free norms. DiTo's LN is closer in spirit to Stable Diffusion's fixed 0.18215 latent scale factor, computed per-sample.

Upstream DeltaTok (third_party/deltatok) — affine, pre-bottleneck, and for a different reason

# models/deltatok.py:51, 75, 112
self.z_embed = nn.Embedding(1, self.backbone.hidden_size)                  # ONE delta token
self.norm    = nn.LayerNorm(self.backbone.hidden_size, cfg.layer_norm_eps) # AFFINE, no bottleneck
...
return self.norm(z)

Three concrete reasons it exists, none of them "latent prior":

  1. z is a regression target. deltaworld.py:82 returns (z_hat, z[:, start:]) and training/world.py:24 uses smooth_l1, whose beta threshold is scale-dependent (training/base.py:313 asserts beta is required). An unnormalized target off a growing pre-norm residual stream would make beta meaningless.
  2. z is concatenated back into a token sequence. decode line 119: hidden = torch.cat((z, x), 1) with x = frozen DINOv3 features, fed to pre-norm blocks. The two halves need comparable scale.
  3. It is the terminal norm of a pre-norm stack — generic hygiene.

Note it is affine (elementwise_affine=True by default), at hidden_size with no bottleneck, over one delta token. We changed all three: non-affine, at z_dim=768 after a bottleneck, across 64 tokens. In our code the terminal-norm role is already filled by pre_bottleneck_norm (affine LN @ 1536, same position, same width) — so self.norm is a second normalization upstream does not have.

The geometry, derived and verified

nn.LayerNorm(768, elementwise_affine=False) on an (M,N,K,768) tensor treats every leading dim as batch, normalizing each of the M·N·K tokens independently:

ẑ_c = (z_c − μ) / √(σ² + ε),   μ = (1/C)Σz_c,   σ² = (1/C)Σ(z_c − μ)²

PyTorch uses the biased (1/C) variance over exactly those C channels, so Σ_c (z_c − μ)² = C·σ² by definition. Therefore

‖ẑ‖² = Σ_c ẑ_c² = C·σ²/(σ² + ε)  ≈  C        →   ‖ẑ‖ ≈ √768 ≈ 27.7128
Σ_c ẑ_c = 0                                  →   ẑ ⊥ 1

Note what is absent from the right-hand side: z itself. The radius depends only on σ²/(σ²+ε), and σ² is z's own variance, so it cancels against itself. Verified over 256 tokens (M·N·K = 2·2·64):

min = max across all 256 tokens, independent of input scale and of the +5.0 offset added to the probe. The std=0.01 row is pure ε: √768 × √(1e−4/1.01e−4) = 27.575, bracketing the measured 27.558–27.594.

Why this fights SIGReg: a genuine N(0,I) draw has ‖z‖² = C ± √(2C) — at C=768 that is 768 ± 39, ~5% radial spread. Our samples have exactly zero spread, so the radial component of the target is unreachable regardless of what the encoder learns. The zero-mean constraint is the milder half: it kills one direction of 768, which random slices hit with weight ~1/C.

Unmeasured. Whether that produces a large floor in the statistic is still open. Each slice sees only the 1-D marginal ⟨z,a⟩, and by Poincaré/Maxwell the marginal of a uniform point on a √C-sphere converges to N(0,1) with O(1/C) error — at C=768 possibly negligible. The synthetic test (our SIGReg on LayerNorm(N(0,I)) vs plain N(0,I) at matched N) was written but not run. If the floor lands near 0.00681 the norm was most of the problem; if ~1e−4 it is a red herring and the recon damage is purely the weight.

What the LayerNorm is NOT responsible for

It does not cause the rank collapse. Participation rank measured immediately before vs after the non-affine LN:

before LNafter LN
linear (no SIGReg)44.5545.24
linear + SIGReg 1.069.4369.81

Unchanged — slightly higher after, in both runs. The collapse had already happened upstream of the norm. This matches the algebra: LN removes 2 dof of 768 per token and zeroes exactly one covariance eigendirection (the all-ones direction), 768 → at most 767. That cannot produce a participation rank of 44.

So there are two distinct problems that were running together: the rank collapse (SIGReg moved it, 44.6 → 69.4, at heavy recon cost) and SIGReg fighting the geometry (the LN's fault). Removing the norm addresses the second only. The expected benefit is not "variance comes back" — it is that SIGReg stops spending gradient on an impossible component.
