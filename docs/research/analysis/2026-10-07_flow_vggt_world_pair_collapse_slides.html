Reading

The two outputs of one call get the same pose

  • Stride 2: frames (2,3), (4,5), (6,7) and (8,9) each come from one call. The second frame of every pair moves only 0.05–0.17 m and turns only 0.05–0.09°.
  • Stride 1: only frames 2 and 3 share a call, and only there is the pair as tight (0.06 m). Frames 4–9 each come from their own call. The odd steps stay small but grow, from 0.19 to 0.62 m.
  • Not explained: why stride 1 still alternates after frame 3. We did not pin down how the pairing carries over.
  • Call 1 is the same in both strides. It gets two real frames, the kind of input stage 1 trains on, and it already collapses.
stride 1 = authors' default (paper appendix B.3) and what our dumps use5
Consequences and caveats — what this does and does not establish

A property of VGGT-World on our benchmark; one decode difference is still untested

What it means

Keep the dumps as they are

  • Nothing to fix in our rollout. Stride 1 is the authors' default and the milder of the two strides.
  • The camera metrics carry it. ATE, RTE, RRE, pointmap and Chamfer all read the predicted poses. The scores are fair for this model on 0.5 s windows.
  • Both checkpoints do it. kitti.pt (stage 1) and cityscapes.pt (stage 2, inferred) have unrelated flow models, so stage-2 training does not remove it.
  • The authors would not have seen it. Their eval scores depth on the last frame only, never pose.
Not tested

What could still move this

  • Our 10-frame decode. We run part2 and the camera head on all 10 frames at once; the authors decode 4. Comparing a call's two output tokens directly would separate the flow model from the decode.
  • Frame spacing. Our frames are 0.5 s apart. VGGT-World trained at 0.2 s (KITTI) and 0.19 s (Cityscapes). That is our benchmark's design, not a wrapper choice.
  • Sample size. Medians over 48 windows per set and 32 nuScenes windows, one noise draw each.
  • Two draws. The 32-window stride test is a different window draw from the 48 on slide 2.
