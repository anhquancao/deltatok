Five threads, and each one changes how the next is read

The tokenizer needs large deltas to learn, the world model needs consecutive frames to generate, and composition is what reconciles them. Once composition is in the objective, the channel-width and regulariser results from before it are no longer the same experiment.

1 · pair sampling

Deltas or nothing

Consecutive frames starve the tokenizer — 2.96× slower. max_gap 1 and 3 collapse the code to rank ~1.9. max_gap 9 adopted.

2 · compose

Costs recon, pays downstream

Tokenizer: +3.0% recon, 3.4× composed recon. Flow: −32% pointmap, −41% raymap.

3 · tc sweep

Width splits by objective

Plain: every doubling helps, tc512 best at 0.0343. Compose: best is tc128, and tc512 costs +83%.

4 · sigreg + compose

The rank knob

Doubling the weight at tc512 compose: −29.6% recon, rank 2.6×. Recon tracks rank at r = −0.99.

5 · long flow run

Peaks, then decays

200k updates. Best at ep 53, then +9.6% / +14.7% worse. And the sampler never integrates.

the dependency that matters
  • Pair sampling and the flow model want opposite things. A delta tokenizer learns from large, varied gaps; the world model only generates when it is fed consecutive frames. The resolution is a consecutive window with pairs drawn at gaps up to 9 inside it — not one or the other.
  • Composition is what makes a span cheap. Train d13 + d35 ≈ d15 and a multi-frame span is a sum in latent space instead of a chain through the decoder. That is the reason to accept a 3% reconstruction loss.
  • Width and regulariser results do not transfer across the objective. Wider is better without compose and worse with it; the same sigreg_weight is right at tc128 and too low at tc512. Every earlier width conclusion has to be re-read under the objective it was measured on.
what is not established
  • One seed per cell, everywhere. No error bars appear anywhere in this deck. Flow geometry metrics carry a 2.6–8.0% within-arm epoch-to-epoch spread, so sub-3% gaps there are not measured.
  • tc512 compose is off-trend against tc768 and does a lot of work in the width headline. Raising its regulariser recovers most of it, which supports "under-regularised" over "bad seed", but the re-run was never done.
  • The long-run decay is one arm. It is the only arm that ran past 100k updates, so "the peak is near 100k" rests on a single curve.

why, and the whole diff

Adjacent frames in a 10 Hz clip move a few centimetres. The delta code has to represent almost nothing, gradients are weak, and the loss drifts down roughly linearly instead of showing a convergence knee.

The sampler never integrates — N steps just move the readout point in t

The arm trains and evaluates at 20 ODE steps, and 20 is the worst of five settings. The reason is algebraic, not a tuning question.

Same checkpoint, five step counts · ringed = best · x categorical
MSEToken
LossPointmap · ceiling 4.06
LossDepth · ceiling 2.79
LossRaymap · ceiling 1.68
the update, reduced
zi+1 = (1 − 1/(N−i))·zi + (1/(N−i))·x̂i

At the last step i = N−1 the mixing weight is exactly 1. So an N-step run returns x̂ evaluated at t = 1 − 1/N, for every N ≤ 20. N=1 is x̂ at t=0, the pure regression baseline; N=20 is x̂ at t=0.95 on a state that is 95% the model's own earlier output.

three causes, ruled in
  • Distortion, not a bug. All four metrics measure distance to one ground truth, whose minimiser is the conditional mean. A sampler that draws a plausible sample pays a variance penalty by construction. "More steps = higher MSE" is the expected direction for a working generative sampler.
  • Ruled out: error amplification (the 1/(1−t) factor cancels against Δt), discretisation error (shrinks with N), and train/eval mismatch in t_shared or context pinning.
  • Measurement bug found while checking. The five passes each drew their own eval t from the global RNG, so the LossFlow column was not comparable across rows. Fixed with a dedicated _eval_t_gen; decoded metrics are byte-unchanged.

5 · long flow training · why even one step is weak, and what the wall actually is

The 1-step readout is the least-trained point in the whole model

The arm trains pred_mode=x with loss_mode=v, which is an x-MSE weighted by 1/clamp(1−t,0.05)². A sample at t=0.95 counts 400× a sample at t=0, so almost no gradient mass lands where the 1-step sampler reads.

gradient mass by t · 4M Monte-Carlo draws, same weight in all rows
schedulet = 0t < 0.1t < 0.5t > 0.95
this arm · uniform0%0.28%2.6%51.3%
logitnormal(−0.7, 1.4)0%1.5%12.6%17.6%
pointdit · +10% forced t=03.6%5.2%60.8%0.02%

So the 0.7417 MSEToken at one step is almost certainly well above the true conditional-mean error, and the 1-vs-20 gap understates how much a properly trained low-t regime would win by.

  • Exposure bias explains the magnitude. Training only ever shows the net a zt built from ground truth plus noise; sampling shows it one built from its own predictions, so error compounds along the trajectory.
  • Per-frame, the ordering does not hold. Of four inspected val sequences only one is monotone in step count; another has 3 steps best and the night scene has 20 best. The aggregate trend is real over 128 sequences but invisible frame by frame.
A sibling codebase already ships both mitigations. pointdit uses the identical Euler update and parameterisation, but starts the ODE from zeros rather than noise, pins 10% of each batch to exactly t=0, and adds an unweighted x-MSE term at weight 0.1. Its released default is 3 steps; nothing there runs 20.

  • Turnover at 0.02 → weight ∝ Cz holds from the tc128 anchor; recalibrate and re-read the whole width sweep under it.
  • Turnover at 0.04 → the rule holds in shape but the tc128 anchor was low, and every arm in the repo was under-regularised.

queued, in dependency order
  • Seed spread at N=1 vs N=20, 8 seeds, plus a decoded depth-edge F1. One debug job. Spread ≈ 0.14 MSEToken at N=20 means the rise is sample variance and the sampler works; much smaller means systematic drift. Without it the distortion story stays unmeasured, and it gates everything below.
  • Fine-tune with the pointdit recipe. Pin 10% of samples to t=0 and add 0.1 × unweighted x-MSE, 10–20k iters from the 200k checkpoint, eval at N=1. Most likely to move the number.
  • Re-run tc512 compose on a new seed to close out the width anomaly, and warm-start compose from the plain tokenizer — compose collapses rank from epoch 4, so hand it a high-rank code instead of asking it to find one. INIT_CKPT is already wired.
  • Set an early-stopping policy for the flow model. The 200k arm's best checkpoint is at ep 53 and nothing currently selects it.
Two open questions with no arm attached. Why compose fails to convert width into rank — the static arithmetic explains at most a third of it. And whether a short-schedule plain tokenizer buys most of compose's composability for free, since plain's own LossRecon_Comp minimum at ep 11 is within 20% of what compose holds for 100 epochs.
