Everything below is at epoch 40, the last epoch tc128 reached before its 24 h wall. The four 40 h arms have run further but are cut to ep 40 so the comparison is matched. Throughput is flat across width — 0.854–0.873 s/it, ~32 min/epoch on all five — so a wider bottleneck costs nothing in wall-clock.

Verdict — no width beats tc128. Keep tc128.
  • Ranking at ep 40 (Eval/Loss): tc128 0.0459 < tc256 0.0469 (+2.2%) < tc768 0.0496 (+8.1%) < tc1024 0.0583 (+27.0%) < tc512 0.0645 (+40.5%). The narrowest code is the best code.
  • tc256 is the only arm that stays close — within 2.4% of tc128 on every metric on both eval sets, and −0.5% on nuScenes LossRaymap. Everything wider is clearly worse.
  • The ordering is not monotone in width. tc512 is the worst arm, below the twice-wider tc1024, on all five metrics and both eval sets.
  • Composition is where the width penalty concentrates. On KITTI, LossRecon_Comp degrades more than LossRecon for every wide arm: +48.7% vs +42.0% at tc512, +33.6% vs +28.1% at tc1024, +9.6% vs +8.7% at tc768. Same sign on nuScenes.
  • Usable rank does not scale with Cz. ZPartRank at ep 40 is 79.6 / 80.9 / 30.6 / 71.1 / 45.3 for tc128 → tc1024 — the same 30–96 band at every budget, so the extra channels go unused: 62% of the budget at tc128 against 4.4% at tc1024. Across the five arms corr(ZPartRank, Eval/Loss) = −0.997.
What this closes: the tc sweep is answered — do not spend more GPU-hours on channel width at K=64. This lines up with the earlier channel-vs-token result: K-reduction is the real compression axis, a channel squeeze is a capacity penalty.

Δ is vs base on the mean of the last 5 evals, ep 95–99 (the last window base has — it lost ep 100 to the SLURM wall). Pointmap / Raymap.

ArmAblated paramValueJob IDStatusResult (last-5 mean)
logitnormt_distlogitnormal44767716DONEworse: +4.4% / +6.5%. Only arm that keeps its sign in every window; loses 32/40 epochs over ep60–99.
What it changes: how the training timestep t is drawn. t is the data fraction (z_t = t·x + (1−t)·e), so t=0 is pure noise and t=1 is clean. Baseline draws t ~ U(0,1) — every noise level equally often. This arm draws t = sigmoid(1.4·N(0,1) − 0.7), median 0.332, concentrating training on the noisy half.
tindept_sharedfalse44767717DONEflat: −0.4% / +0.5%, inside the window spread.
What it changes: whether the two forecast slots share a timestep. Baseline draws one t per sample and broadcasts it to both, so the whole clip sits at one noise level. This arm draws an independent t per slot, so frame 3 can be nearly clean while frame 4 is nearly noise.
cosineschedsampler_scheduler_modecosine44767718DONEflat: +0.7% / +0.7%. Was −1.3% / −2.8% on the ep60–99 mean — sign flips by window, so unresolved.
What it changes: where the 20 ODE steps land in t at eval. Baseline is a uniform grid (progress = i/N). Cosine uses 1 − cos(progress·π/2), which starts slow, so more steps fall near t=0 — the noisy end where the velocity field turns fastest. Decode-time only, never touches training.
alpha05sampler_alpha0.544767719DONE+2.6% / +5.2% here, but +0.9% / +1.8% on ep96–100 and +0.4% / +0.1% on ep60–99. Window noise, not a regression.
What it changes: per-frame stagger while sampling. Each view integrates at t = progress · (1 + offset·alpha) with offset running 1→0 across views (occrae/flow_matching.py:214), so the earliest forecast frame runs 50% ahead and finishes denoising first. alpha=0 collapses to one shared schedule. Decode-time only.

The ±spread is the size of the gaps being compared, which is why only logitnorm resolves. Rank on the 40-eval ep60–99 mean in the journal write-up.

Decision — keep the baseline. No config change. 
model.t_dist=uniform
model.t_shared=true
model.sampler_scheduler_mode=linear
model.sampler_alpha=0
  • Drop logitnormal permanently. The only resolved effect, and negative in every window. Mechanism is understood: mu=-0.7 puts t near the noise end while loss_mode=v weights t→1, so it under-trains exactly where the loss cares.
  • t_shared=true stays. Flat empirically, and it is the matched training prior for sampler_alpha=0, where all slots share one t.
  • linear stays for the same reason — a uniform-in-t grid matches a uniform-t prior. cosine flips sign with the window, so there is nothing to act on.
  • sampler_alpha=0 stays. 0.5 is noise, not a measured cost, but also not a reason to add stagger.
Cheap follow-up: sampler_scheduler_mode and sampler_alpha are decode-time only (occrae/deltatok_flow_trainer.py:791-792). Sweep both on the final base checkpoint with eval_deltatok_flow_sampler.py — minutes, no training RNG, and it settles them properly. Switching costs nothing since neither touches training.

Where the headroom actually is: the flow sits ×2.15 Pointmap and ×3.51 Raymap above the tokenizer ceiling, and no t-schedule knob moved that ratio by more than 0.23. Sections 2, 3 and 6 below are the better spend.
