the comparison — one recipe, three ways of wiring the noise

One variable between the arm and its twin: where the noised decode's gradient goes.

All three are the same tc512 compose tokenizer at sigreg_weight 0.02, pool 8192, ns 1024. The twin has no noise term at all. The sibling puts z + σ·ε in the encoder's graph. The arm decodes z.detach() + σ·ε a second time, so the encoder's gradients are the twin's exactly and only the decoder sees the noise.

the decoded read — PredVsOrig, the tokenizer's own round trip

On KITTI the noised decoder decodes geometry better than the clean one.

At matched ep 82 the arm beats its twin on KITTI depth (−4.1%) and pointmap (−4.4%) while losing 2.7% on the token loss the decode comes from. Raymap is the exception and loses on both sets (+7.5% / +4.4%). On nuScenes depth and pointmap go the other way by 2–3%. So the noise does not simply cost — it redistributes, and the token loss is the wrong single number to rank these two arms by.

the noise ladder over training — absolute LossRecon at each sigma, every epoch

The tolerance stops improving at ep 60. The clean branch does not.

The absolute LossRecon, decoded at each σ. From ep 60 to ep 100 on KITTI the σ=0.82 curve moves 0.0671 → 0.0661 (−1.5%) while the clean curve moves 0.0544 → 0.0520 (−4.4%): the noised decode has flattened and the clean one has not. Robustness converges ~40 epochs before clean accuracy does. Any ratio of the two therefore widens on its own — the σ=0.82 penalty goes +17.5% at ep 40 to +27.1% at ep 100 — so a ladder read late flatters the twin, and a twin comparison has to be epoch-matched.
