Findings

The tolerance is real on isotropic noise and does not reach the flow.

1
More steps only hurt tc512. Raymap 4.60 / 4.58 / 5.18 / 5.82 / 6.38 at N = 1 / 2 / 4 / 8 / 20 — the tc128 source decoder's shape, not ft10's.
2
Its decoders are flat on the ladder. At σ = 0.82 detached raymap moves −0.3% and the clean twin +11%, against +1,039% for the tc128 source. The isotropic ladder cannot separate tc512 from ft10 on raymap.
3
Best against best, ft10 at tc128 still wins: tc512 is +20.7% raymap and +5.8% pointmap. At N ≤ 2 tc512 matches or beats both tc128 decoders.
4
The flow error grows faster on tc512: MSEToken +4.1% over tc128 at N=1, +12.6% at N=20. At matched training epoch the decoded losses tie.
5
An earlier claim is retracted. The KITTI token ladder suggested the tc512 decoder was less tolerant than ft10. Decoded on Waymo it is the flatter one on all three losses.
Untested reading. σ is absolute, and at tc512 σ² = 0.64 is 8–12% of the per-channel signal against 35–50% at tc128. The decoder never saw errors of the flow's size in the channels it reads (slide 05).
Caveat. These sweeps are 1-GPU evals. The tc512 N=20 point reads MSEToken 0.9968 / raymap 6.38 against the run's own 4-GPU ep-49 log 0.9954 / 6.19.
