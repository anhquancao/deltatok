Findings

Full width trains and wins at N = 20. Whether it wins best-against-best needs a step sweep.

1
It trains. LossFlow 0.780 → 0.495 and MSEToken 1.77 → 1.10 over ep 0–43, no NaN, no errors. LossFlow has been flat since ep ~25 (0.498 → 0.495).
2
It beats both at matched epoch. Ep 39–43 against tc128 / tc512: depth −3.5% / −6.0%, pointmap −6.8% / −15.2%, raymap −6.5% / −17.1%.
3
It is already past tc128's best. tc128's lowest 5-epoch window in 100 epochs is ep 40–44 (pointmap 8.69, raymap 6.02); tc1536 at ep 43 is 5.9% and 5.2% under it. tc128 then drifts up to 9.16 / 6.42 by ep 99.
4
Depth is the tokenizer; pointmap and raymap are the flow. Above each ceiling, tc1536 vs tc128 is depth +1.2%, pointmap −8.7%, raymap −7.8%.
5
It reverses the 2026-08-18 tc sweep, which ranked tc1536 worst. That flow (BSC:44543763) read pointmap 14.9 / raymap 13.5 at 3.2 M samples; this one reads 9.0 / 6.6 at the same count. Different tokenizer (SIGReg 0.005, pool 8192, ep 48) and batch 32, so not matched.
Caveat · steps. Every number here is N = 20. The tc128 and tc512 flows read best at N = 1–2 offline (tc512 raymap 4.58 at N = 2 vs 6.38 at N = 20). Nothing says tc1536 keeps its lead there.
Caveat · tokenizer. Not a pure width ablation: bottleneck, SIGReg weight, pool, warmup and decoder noise also differ (slide 02). tc1536 reads epoch_30, the others epoch_100.
Caveat · MSEToken. It is in each tokenizer's own z units and ranks the widths opposite to every decoded loss. It was not normalised by code variance.
Caveat · one seed per arm, and the 128 eval sequences are fixed across epochs.
Frames do not show the win (slides 06–07). On the 8 logged scenes at t4, depth error against own GT-z is 2.20 / 2.37 / 2.24 m (tc128 / tc512 / tc1536); tc128 is lowest on 6 of 8.
