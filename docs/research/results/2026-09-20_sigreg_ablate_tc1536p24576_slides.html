the trap — why the noise ladder is not a robustness read

The no-SIGReg arm looks 76% more noise-robust. It is not — σ is absolute and its z is 18× larger.

training.eval_noise_sigmas is a fixed list [0.32, 0.55, 0.82] added to z. Per element, the control's z has RMS √1.12 = 1.06 and the no-SIGReg arm's has √343.7 = 18.5. The same σ is a 77.5% perturbation on one arm and a 4.4% perturbation on the other.

the read — question 23, tokenizer half

SIGReg is not paying for itself on reconstruction. Whether it is paying for the flow is the untested half.

1
The recon cost of SIGReg is real and late. It is a 6.5% help at ep 35 and a 10% tax by ep 72. Anything read before ep 49 gets the sign wrong.
2
SIGReg does deliver scale and rank. Row RMS 1.12 vs 344, and rank 197 / 244 vs 149 / 130. It is doing exactly what it claims — the question is what that is worth downstream.
3
Rank without SIGReg peaks early and decays. 178 at ep 13, 149 at ep 72. Better recon is coming from somewhere other than a wider code.
4
The noise ladder is a scale artifact on this pair. Both arms' σ rows are unusable; a relative ladder is not logged.
5
No train-side z telemetry. The Train/Z* probe is nested under the SIGReg branch, so the ablation arm cannot be diagnosed from train stats at all.
