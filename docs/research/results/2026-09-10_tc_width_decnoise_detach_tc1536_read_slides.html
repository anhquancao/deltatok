verdict

The bottleneck was the cost. The noise recipe is a new one.

  • Removing the tc512 bottleneck halves the loss and tc512 cannot close it with 2× the epochs — its ep 61 (0.0542) is still 1.83× this arm's ep 31.
  • But the plain tc1536 arm beats this one at every epoch. So the width buys the 2×; the decnoise + 12× sigreg recipe gives 17–25% of it back.
  • The z code is stuck in a state the plain arm escaped at ep 4 — see slides 5 and 6. SIGReg reports success throughout and cannot see it.

z spread — participation ratio and row scale · both tc1536 arms enter the same state

The plain arm escapes at ep 4. Neither tc1536 arm is stable.

ZPartRank · effective dims of Cz · solid KITTI, dashed nuScenes
ZRowMeanSquare · log axis · dashed rule = the SIGReg anchor, 1.0
This is a shared transient, not a width property — and at tc1536 nothing normalises z. pre_bottleneck_norm and z_proj_down are built only when z_dim != hidden_size (deltatok_trainer.py:151), and all three arms run z_norm=false, which makes the final norm an Identity (:184). So tc512 keeps a LayerNorm pinning z_proj_down’s input to unit variance and tc1536 has no norm on z at all — SIGReg is its only anchor. Both tc1536 arms therefore blow up during warmup, while the sigreg ramp is still near 0. The plain arm snaps back at ep 4 (PR 1.0 → 44.2, row scale 177 → 1.36); the detach arm gets to 26 and then climbs away again to 872 (peak 5069 at ep 21). But the plain arm is only un-blown-up, not stable: past ep 10 its rank swings 16.4% epoch-to-epoch against tc512’s 2.4%, it ends at 72% of its own peak (163 at ep 43 vs 279 at ep 30) while tc512 is at 97% and still climbing, and its channel offset rises monotonically 0.59 → 2.37 while tc512’s falls. Only its row scale is anchored. tc512 is the one arm here with a stable code, and the one arm with a norm on z. Which knob drives the detach arm out — sigreg 0.06 vs 0.005 or the detached noise decode — this read cannot say.

The runaway direction is axis-concentrated, not random. Per-channel std = √λ₁·|uc| for the top direction u, and √λ₁ = 1086, so the peak channel carries |uc| = 0.605. A random unit vector in 1536-d reaches at most ~0.098 — the measured channel is 6.2× past that, with the median channel 7× below random. One channel, plus a sparse handful above p99.
is it a bug? four checks say no

One is an average, the other is a second moment.

  • Confirmed on the checkpoint, not just in the logs. An independent --full read at epoch_30.pth reproduces every scalar. ZRowMeanSquare is also accumulated off the covariance path entirely (z_spread.py:48, no eigvalsh) and agrees.
  • Not undersampling either. 51 200 rows against Cz = 1536 is 33 per dimension, and the train accumulator banks 1.67M (1085/dim) for the same reading. The ceiling is 1491, so a healthy code reads ~1491, not 1.
And SIGReg cannot pull it back — escape radius |p| ≈ 100. Its restoring force stays coherent for |p| ≲ 30, but a ±5% jitter in the radius cancels 97.5% of it at |p| = 100 and 99.99% at 1000 — the phase wraps faster than an optimiser step. Past it a row is free whatever sigreg_weight says.
!
The tail must be a small fraction of ROWS. Mean per-channel variance is 774, so a random slice projection has std 27.8 — if every row sat there SIGReg would read 1.26. It reads 0.0014. That is only possible if the variance comes from a sparse set of rows: the measurement forces the account, it does not weaken it.

Decoder noise ladder — eval-time z + σ·ε, penalty relative to each arm's own σ = 0

The one thing the recipe is for, it does worse.

what this measures

Tolerance to latent error, the flow's failure mode.

  • tc512 detach degrades +15.2% (KITTI) and +18.0% (nuScenes) at σ = 0.82.
  • tc1536 detach degrades +62.5% and +66.5% — 4.1× and 3.7× the twin's penalty, at the same τ = 0.8 and the same detached recipe.
  • Absolute loss is still lower at every σ (0.0481 vs 0.0704 at σ = 0.82, KITTI). It is the slope that inverts.
  • The plain tc1536 arm has no ladder — it was trained before decode_noise_tau existed.
A code whose rows span 100 to 104 cannot be robust to a fixed absolute σ: the same σ is negligible for the tail rows and overwhelming for the bulk. This is the "σ must go relative" finding from the ep-37 read, arriving by a second route.
Caveat. Both arms are read at ep 31 from their own in-run eval, not from a shared --eval-only pass on matched checkpoints with one seeded ε — so treat the ratio as the sign and the size, not a precise figure.

Reading — what this settles, what it does not, and the guard the plan asked for

Three findings, one of them the plan did not ask about.

answered

The plan's two questions.

  • Does the noise-tolerant decoder hold at full width? no The ladder is 3.7–4.1× steeper than at tc512 (slide 7).
  • Does ∝ Cz put sigreg 0.06 inside the plateau at 1536? guard passes The tc512 0.08 break signature does not appear: train recon is 0.51× the twin's, not 1.8×, and the raw SIGReg residual descends monotonically to 0.0014.
  • But the guard was the wrong instrument. It passes while z sits at PR 1.0 — both of its conditions are ones SIGReg can see, and this failure is one it cannot.
not answered

What this read cannot separate.

  • sigreg 0.06 vs the decnoise term. Both differ from the plain tc1536 arm. The clean split is a tc1536 detach arm at sigreg 0.005, or a plain tc1536 arm at 0.06.
  • Whether the tail is really sparse. Slide 6 is a simulation that fits three scalars, not a checkpoint read. compute_deltatok_z_spread.py with full=True settles it in one short job.
  • ep 31 is not convergence. tc512 ran to 61 and the plain arm to 43; this arm is still training, and BSC:45665286 is queued to extend tc512.
→
The headline is that the tc512 bottleneck costs ~2× in recon, and that removing it is worth more than anything on the sigreg or noise axes so far. The headline is not that this arm is the new best — the 2026-08 plain tc1536 run beats it at every epoch on every readout measured here, without a noise term and at 1/12 the sigreg weight. Before another tc1536 arm is launched, the PR = 1 state needs a mechanism, and SIGReg is not the tool that will find it.
