Part 2 — SIGReg: from a per-window pool to a rolling queue

The prior _pool estimator banked the z rows of one grad-accum window and ran the statistic once, on the last micro-batch, multiplying by grad_cum to undo the (loss_total/grad_cum).backward() divisor. The new form keeps a FIFO queue of detached rows from recent micro-batches and runs the estimator every micro-batch over live + queue.

Why fire every time

  • The scaling disappears. A term present in every micro-batch is divided by grad_cum and appears grad_cum times — the divisor cancels, exactly as it does for recon. No compensation factor needed.
  • No cross-rank vote. A "has the pool reached its target" test would differ across ranks (N = 1..2 cameras varies per sample), and sigreg.py all-reduces the CF inside its forward — a split decision would deadlock. Firing unconditionally keeps ranks in lockstep for free.
  • No burstiness. One large kick every P steps against grad_clip=0.1 is replaced by a steady push.

Why scale = pool/live

The statistic is a mean over pooled rows (cos_mean = x_t.cos().mean(0)), so a single row moves it by 1/Q:

∂cos_mean/∂s_i = (1/Q) · ∂cos(t_j⟨s_i,a_k⟩)/∂s_i   ⇒   ∂L/∂s_i ∝ 1/Q

Only the S live rows carry gradient, so total pressure ∝ S/Q — i.e. raising the pool to escape the finite-sample floor would quietly throttle the very pressure you raised it to make meaningful, confounding Q with sigreg_weight. Multiplying by pool/live cancels it, leaving Q a pure estimator-quality knob.

The pressure change — derived

Let S = live rows/rank, D = per-row CF-derivative magnitude, w = sigreg_weight.

OLD  fires once/window, coeff w·grad_cum, ÷grad_cum
     per step = w · S·D / (grad_cum·S)          =  w·D / grad_cum

NEW  fires every µbatch, coeff w·scale, ÷grad_cum,  scale = S_pool/S
     per µbatch = (w·scale/grad_cum) · S·D/S_pool =  w·D / grad_cum
     × grad_cum µbatches per step                =  w·D
⇒ NEW = grad_cum × OLD. At grad_cum=2 (both the BSC and JZ layouts), the same SIGREG_WEIGHT=1.0 is 2× the pressure of the earlier arms. Note the old form scaled as 1/grad_cum, so SIGReg strength silently tracked the cluster layout; the new form does not. Chosen deliberately — but it must not masquerade as backward-compatible, so the docstring and train_deltatok.yaml both say so.

Review outcome

An independent review confirmed the mechanism: no row double-counted or dropped (pooled is built before live.detach() is banked), eviction terminates and cannot empty the queue, autograd routes gradient only to live rows with no graph retained across iterations, memory bounded (~13 MB/rank), no deadlock path, and scale = pool/live is the right factor (exponent 1, not ½). The no_sync() trap noted at sigreg.py:80 does not apply — this trainer never uses it. Its one substantive catch was the grad_cum× pressure claim above, which was re-derived independently before acting on it.

Also simplified out of the first draft: a sigreg_pool_samples=0 "legacy depth" branch that was neither pressure- nor depth-compatible with the old code, and a per-micro-batch seed term that would have made rank-synchronisation depend on num_batches matching everywhere — iter alone is the one counter guaranteed equal on every rank, which the CF all-reduce requires.

Watch list

  1. Does it run at all. Answered by the smoke test above.
  2. Train/LossSIGReg should sit well above its finite-sample floor and descend. Flat ⇒ the estimator is still under-resolved, which is what sank job 168363. The smoke run was far too short to judge this; the rolling-queue estimator remains unproven.
  3. The z-spread triple, read together. Eval/<loader>/ZPartRank is the headline — does it clear the ≈332/1536 that uncompressed dtok64 reached without SIGReg? But ZPartRank weights the strong axes, so a regularizer can flatten the leading eigenvalues and lift it while the tail stays dead. ZEntRank (credits the weak tail) and ZN90 (literal dims for 90% of variance) are what distinguish genuine spreading from redistribution inside the subspace already in use. All are computed on the raw covariance — never quote a whitened participation ratio, per-channel rescaling nearly doubles it.
  4. Train/LossRecon is the cross-arm comparison metric — unaffected by SIGReg weighting. LossTot deliberately still includes the weighted SIGReg term, so do not compare it across arms. Eval/Loss is pure recon on val and is likewise unaffected.
  5. Gradient skips. 2× pressure against grad_clip=0.1: if clipping dominates or [warn] skipping optim step appears, attribute it here first. None in the first 320 iters, but sigreg_warmup=2000 means full pressure had not yet arrived.

At ~40 min/epoch, 48h buys roughly 70 of the config's 100 epochs, so expect to chain a resume (--dependency=afterany:43972396 --export=ALL,RESUME=1, or the chain-slurm-jobs skill). training.exit_before_time_limit=true makes that safe.
