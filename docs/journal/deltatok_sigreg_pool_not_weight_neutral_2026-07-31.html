sigreg_pool_samples is not a pure estimator knob

Raising the SIGReg pool 8192 → 32768 at an unchanged sigreg_weight stopped DeltaTok learning: Eval/Loss sat at its untrained value through epoch 11 while the matched pool8192 twin was already at 0.033 by epoch 4. The suspicion was that the no_grad refactor in aba8f00 — landed in the same commit — had broken the gradient. It had not. The cause is the pool size itself, through the scale = n_pooled / n_live compensation and the feedback delay a longer FIFO introduces.

TL;DR

  • The no_grad refactor is inert — verified, not assumed. Ran the pre-aba8f00 single-concat path against the current forward(live, pool, seed) on BSC's own deployed occrae/sigreg.py at real shapes: value bit-identical, gradient max abs diff 2.8e-13, pool.grad is None. It is a pure memory optimization, confounded with pool size only because it is what made pool=32768 fit.
  • pool silently multiplies the pressure. scale = n_pooled / n_live goes 11.7 → 43.7, and the FIFO grows from ~5 to ~21 optimizer steps of frozen history.
  • The statistic diverges at the warmup boundary. Train/LossSIGReg spikes 39× (0.019 → 0.748) at iter 2211 — exactly where sigreg_warmup=2000 ends — then stays ~10× above the pool8192 twin for the whole run. SIGReg never reaches its own fixed point, the opposite of the intent.
  • Every weight is stuck, monotonically worse. ep11 Eval/Loss at pool32768: w=0.05 .1016, w=0.5 .1188, w=5 .1151. That is the fingerprint of excess pressure, not a broken estimator.
  • Action. BSC 44014668 reruns pool=32768 at w=0.01 ≈ 0.05 × 11.7/43.7. If it recovers the JZ trajectory, the real fix is to cap scale in _sigreg_pooled so pool becomes the pure estimator-quality knob its docstring already claims to be.

The two arms are otherwise identical

Both are 4 ranks × bsize 2 × grad_cum 2 (effective_bsize=16), 1125 iters/epoch, lr=1e-3, grad_clip=0.1, sigreg_warmup=2000, ns=3072, tc1536, dtok64, z_norm=false. The printed EXTRA_CFG differs in sigreg_pool_samples alone. Different clusters, but the layout the estimator depends on matches.

Why the pool changes the pressure

_sigreg_pooled (occrae/deltatok_trainer.py) sets scale = n_pooled / n_live, with live L = M·N·K = 2·(1..2)·64 ≈ 192 and cap_local = pool / world:

cap_localscaleFIFO span
pool81922048≈11.7 ~11 micro-batches ≈ 5 optimizer steps
pool327688192≈43.7 ~43 micro-batches ≈ 21 optimizer steps

scale does exactly what its docstring says: it cancels the instantaneous 1/n_pooled attenuation, so the per-live-row gradient magnitude is pool-independent. What it does not cancel is the feedback delay. The residual (cos_mean − φ) that drives the gradient is now ~98% determined by frozen history the model cannot move within its own reaction time, so the SIGReg gradient stays near full magnitude roughly 4× longer before it relaxes. With grad_clip=0.1, SIGReg then dominates the global gradient norm and clipping rescales the recon component down proportionally — which is the direct route from "SIGReg term 4× bigger" to "recon does not learn at all".

Measured vs inferred. The scale arithmetic, the 39× spike, the 10× statistic gap and the Eval/Loss divergence are measured. The grad_clip starvation step is the mechanism that best fits them, but the pre-clip gradient norm is not currently logged per term, so it is inferred rather than confirmed. Splitting ||g_recon|| from ||g_sigreg|| before clipping would settle it.

What does not change

The 1/S argument that motivated pool=32768 in the first place still stands: at S=8192 the finite-sample floor is 1.32e-4, ~80% of the 1.65e-4 anisotropy signal, an O(1/S) bias in the fixed point that 32768 drops to 3.2e-5. That is a statement about estimator bias and this entry does not contradict it. The flaw is that raising S also raises scale and the delay, and those were not held constant — so the intended clean experiment was never actually run.

What to watch on BSC 44014668

  1. Iter 2000–2500 first. Train/LossSIGReg must descend through the warmup boundary. Anything resembling the 0.019 → 0.748 excursion means w=0.01 is still too hot and the next rung is 0.005. Floor to stay above: ~3.2e-5; flat at the floor means under-resolved instead.
  2. Epoch 3–4. Eval/Loss should be heading toward .059/.033 like the JZ twin, not parked at .123.
  3. Then fix the knob, not the weight. A successful w=0.01 run confirms the diagnosis but leaves pool and sigreg_weight coupled. Capping scale in _sigreg_pooled is what makes prior arms weight-comparable again.

Extraction gotcha. Train/LossSIGReg, LossRecon and LossTot are logged per micro-batch, so several values share a step — do not back out an exact scale from LossTot − LossRecon. These are tensor summaries, not simple_value; decode with tensor_util.make_ndarray or every point reads 0.
